# ADR-0023 — The phone is a consent surface

**Status:** accepted; **superseded in part 2026-08-09 by [ADR-0028](0028-trust-is-a-mode-and-the-default-asks-almost-nothing.md)**.
**Date:** 2026-08-08
**Follows from [ADR-0022](0022-failure-to-act-is-harm-we-caused.md). Constrained by [ADR-0007](0007-identity-is-derived-credentials-are-minted.md).**

> **Which part.** The phone is re-framed from a **checkpoint that must be passed** to a
> place where a question can be **answered**. Its mechanism and authority model are
> unchanged — the decision travels with the person, the authority never leaves the hub.
> It no longer implies a gate on the path. Q11 also settled that this needs no new
> subsystem: the runtime's `ResolutionPolicy` is a swappable interface, so a phone-routed
> policy is an implementation of an existing seam. Q18 names the proof-of-human standard
> (WebAuthn) and its honest limit — it proves possession plus local unlock, not identity.
**Forward decision. The prototype had a phone page; it had no remote consent.**

## Context

[ADR-0022](0022-failure-to-act-is-harm-we-caused.md) requires that a wall reaches the person wherever they are. Reaching them is half a solution — the other half is that they can *do something about it* from where they are standing. Otherwise the notification reads "your task is stuck, go home", which is an improvement over silence and not much of one.

Jamie's addition, 2026-08-08: the message says what the agent wants to do and which permission it needs, the person can grant it from the phone, and — the more interesting half — they can steer the agent in a different direction if they disagree.

This lands on a rule in [01-ARCHITECTURE.md §2](../01-ARCHITECTURE.md) that must not be quietly broken: **clients hold no authority; a client asks, the hub decides, the daemon acts.** A phone that grants permissions looks like a violation of that rule and is not, provided the distinction is kept exact. The phone does not *hold* authority. It *carries the person's decision* to the hub. The authority is the human's, and it always was — the phone is a terminal for it, in the same way the dashboard is. What the hub must never do is trust the claim; it derives who is on the other end, which is [ADR-0007](0007-identity-is-derived-credentials-are-minted.md) unchanged.

Three hazards make this sharper than it first appears.

**The agent would be describing its own request.** A permission prompt says what the agent wants to do, and the agent is the thing supplying that description. That is a hole with real consequences on the other side of it: a confused or manipulated agent writes a reassuring sentence about reading a notes application while requesting submit authority on a mail client, and the person taps yes to something they did not read. This is the same principle as attestation in [ADR-0008](0008-scopes-operation-classes-and-honest-refusals.md) — *the thing being authorised does not get to author the authorisation* — and the prototype already had a version of it in amendment A15, approval criteria the agent cannot author.

**A grant given under time pressure is not the same as one given at rest.** Someone standing in a shop whose task has stalled is mildly stressed and will tap yes. That is approval fatigue arriving through a side door, immediately after [ADR-0021](0021-standing-authority-is-armable-attestation-is-not-waivable.md) removed it from the front.

**Device possession is not human presence.** An unlocked phone in a pocket proves nothing about whether a person is present and paying attention. What is wanted at the moment of a consequential grant is proof that a human was *there, then, for this*. A biometric or PIN challenge provides that; a notification tap does not. Jamie proposed making this configurable, which is right — the level should scale with consequence, and the scope ladder already orders consequence.

## Decision

1. **The phone is a consent surface, not an authority.** It carries the person's decision. The hub derives identity ([ADR-0007](0007-identity-is-derived-credentials-are-minted.md)) and makes every decision. [01-ARCHITECTURE.md §2](../01-ARCHITECTURE.md) is intact.
2. **The request shown to the person is generated by the hub from the structured ask** — this application, this scope, this consequence. It is never assembled from agent prose.
3. **The agent gets exactly one field, clearly attributed as the agent's own words**, to say why it wants the permission. It is visibly the agent talking, and it can never be mistaken for the sentence being agreed to.
4. **Proof-of-human scales with consequence, and is configurable by the person.** Defaults follow the scope ladder: `observe` and `edit`/`activate` may be a tap; `submit` and `destructive`, and arming any standing authority ([ADR-0021](0021-standing-authority-is-armable-attestation-is-not-waivable.md)), require a biometric or PIN challenge. The person may raise these; lowering the top rung below proof-of-human is not offered.
5. **Remote grants are task-scoped by default.** The default action is *allow this, for this task*. Standing authority is granted at the dashboard, sitting down, where the [ADR-0021](0021-standing-authority-is-armable-attestation-is-not-waivable.md) rule-4 warning can actually be read. Two surfaces, two defaults: just-in-time and narrow on the phone, deliberate and durable at the desk.
6. **Declining is a first-class answer and does not end the task.** A decline returns to the agent as information, and the agent proceeds with what it still has authority to do — consistent with [ADR-0022](0022-failure-to-act-is-harm-we-caused.md).
7. **Every remote grant is in the audit log** with the surface it came from, the proof-of-human level that was met, and whether it was task-scoped or standing.
8. **This does not schedule the phone client's visual design.** It moves the *consent and notification path* onto the critical path for M6. Everything else about the phone stays deferred.

## Consequences

**Good.** It completes [ADR-0022](0022-failure-to-act-is-harm-we-caused.md). A wall becomes a thing the person clears in ten seconds from a shop, rather than a thing that ends the task.

**Good.** Hub-generated requests make the consent text reviewable and testable — it is generated from the structured ask, so a test can assert that what a person sees matches what was actually requested. Agent prose could never have been tested that way.

**Cost.** A remote-grant path is new attack surface on the most valuable operation in the system. It inherits [ADR-0007](0007-identity-is-derived-credentials-are-minted.md)'s derivation rules and needs its own mutation tests; it is the highest-value target in the product and should be treated as such.

**Cost.** Task-scoped-by-default means a person who genuinely wants a standing grant must do it twice — once now, properly later at the dashboard. That friction is deliberate and it will be complained about.

**Cost.** Proof-of-human depends on the device, and a person without biometrics or a PIN cannot reach the top of the ladder from their phone. The honest consequence is that some grants are only available at the desk. Better than pretending a tap is a person.

**Cost.** Notification and consent on the critical path for M6 is more scope in the milestone that already carries the north star sentence. Accepted: [ADR-0022](0022-failure-to-act-is-harm-we-caused.md) makes it a safety property, and safety properties are not deferrable to a polish milestone.
